AI-Powered Clip Detection

This is the core of what Reka Clip does, and it is worth understanding how it actually works. The AI analyzes your video at multiple levels — transcribing the speech, identifying key topics, detecting changes in energy and pacing, recognizing emphasis patterns, and evaluating the overall narrative arc of the content. From that analysis, it identifies segments that are likely to stand alone as compelling, watchable short pieces.

The practical result is that you do not have to decide where to start and stop a clip. The AI presents you with a set of already-extracted moments, each with a score or ranking reflecting how compelling it determined the segment to be. Your job becomes reviewing and approving rather than discovering and extracting. That shift — from manual search to curated review — is where the time savings actually come from.

For conversational content like podcasts and interviews, this detection tends to work particularly well. The AI is good at finding moments where an idea comes to a natural and satisfying conclusion — the kind of self-contained thought that does not need surrounding context to make sense. For more visual or tightly scripted content, the detection may require a bit more editorial input from you before publishing.

Automatic Highlight Extraction

Once the AI identifies your best moments, Reka Clip handles the extraction automatically. It trims the clip to the appropriate start and end points, handles the transition in and out, and packages each segment as a standalone piece of video. You are not doing any manual cutting — the clip arrives already trimmed based on the AI's analysis of where the moment naturally begins and ends.

This matters more than it might seem at first. One of the most time-consuming parts of manual clipping is finding the right in-and-out points — starting a clip slightly too early captures a lot of silence and awkward setup, while ending it slightly too late loses the momentum. Getting those beats right takes judgment and repeated playback. The AI does this automatically and gets it right at a level that requires minimal correction in most cases.

Viral Moment Identification

Reka Clip is specifically designed around the idea of virality — not in a vague aspirational sense, but in a technically concrete one. The AI is trained to recognize patterns associated with high-engagement short-form content: strong hooks, clear takeaways, quotable statements, emotional peaks, and moments of genuine insight or surprise. These are the qualities that drive replays, shares, and comments on short-form platforms.

This distinction is important. A generic clip extractor might give you random segments of your video. Reka Clip is attempting to give you the segments most likely to perform — to stop someone mid-scroll, get watched to the end, and be shared. Whether its judgment consistently matches your audience's response is something you will only know by testing, but the intent and architecture of the analysis is built around performance rather than just extraction.

Smart Captions

Captions are no longer optional for social media video. Studies consistently show that a majority of social media video is watched without sound, and uncaptioned content gets scrolled past by viewers who cannot or choose not to use audio. Reka Clip generates captions automatically from the speech in your video, formatting them to appear in sync with the audio in a way that is readable on mobile screens.

Beyond accessibility, captions also increase the discoverability of your content on platforms that use text from captions for search and recommendation. A clip with accurate captions is essentially captioning itself for the platform's algorithm. For creators who have been skipping captions because of the time required to add them manually, this feature alone can meaningfully change their clip performance.

Social-Media-Ready Formatting

Every social media platform has its own requirements for video dimensions, aspect ratios, and file specifications. TikTok and Instagram Reels want vertical 9:16 video. YouTube Shorts has its own requirements. LinkedIn and Twitter have different optimal formats again. Resizing and reformatting a single clip for all of these destinations manually — especially after you have already produced multiple clips from a single video — adds up quickly.

Reka Clip handles platform-specific formatting automatically at export. You tell the tool which platforms you are publishing to and it outputs clips in the appropriate dimensions for each. You do not need to know the exact pixel dimensions of a TikTok video or manually reframe a horizontal clip for vertical display. The AI handles intelligent reframing to keep the speaker in focus when switching between aspect ratios, which is a meaningful quality-of-life feature for anyone publishing across multiple platforms.

Speaker Tracking

For interview content or multi-speaker podcasts, speaker tracking keeps the video focused on whoever is speaking at any given moment. This is particularly valuable when reframing horizontal content to vertical format — without speaker tracking, a static crop of a wide shot might leave the active speaker off-center or partially out of frame, making the clip look unpolished.

Speaker tracking analyzes where the speaker is in the frame throughout the clip and dynamically adjusts the crop to keep them centered and appropriately framed. The result is a vertical clip that looks like it was intentionally shot for vertical consumption rather than awkwardly cropped from a wide-angle recording. For podcasters who record on camera or use video conferencing setups, this makes a real difference in the presentation quality of their clips.

How Reka Clip Works

The workflow is genuinely straightforward, which is one of the reasons the platform works well even for creators who are not technically inclined. Here is how it flows from start to published clip.

Step 1 — Upload Your Video: You start by uploading your long-form video directly to Reka Clip. Depending on the platform's current integrations, you may also be able to connect via URL for content already published to YouTube or other hosting platforms. Supported formats include the common video file types used by cameras, screen recorders, and video conferencing software.

Step 2 — AI Analyzes Your Content: Once uploaded, the AI begins processing the video. It transcribes the audio, analyzes the content structure, identifies speaker patterns, evaluates energy levels throughout the recording, and builds a picture of where the most compelling and self-contained moments occur. This happens automatically without any input from you during processing.

Step 3 — Highlights Are Detected and Ranked: After processing, Reka Clip presents you with a set of identified highlights. Each suggested clip typically shows you the transcript of the segment, a preview of the video, and some indication of why the AI selected it — what made it stand out as a candidate for a short clip. Step 4 — Clips Are Generated: The platform automatically generates the actual short clips from the identified moments — trimmed, formatted, and with captions applied. You are not just getting timestamps and having to do the cutting yourself; the clips arrive already packaged.

Step 5 — Customize Your Output: Before exporting, you have the ability to make adjustments. This might include tweaking the start and end points of a clip, editing caption text for accuracy or emphasis, adjusting the caption styling, reordering elements, or selecting which clips to take forward and which to discard. You stay in control of what actually gets published.

Step 6 — Export and Publish: Select your target platforms, choose the appropriate format for each, and export. The clips come out formatted and ready to upload directly to TikTok, Instagram, YouTube Shorts, LinkedIn, or wherever you publish. No further reformatting required — just upload and schedule.

Tips for Getting Better Viral Clips

Even the best AI clip generator produces better results when you approach it intentionally. Here are practical things that make a real difference in clip quality and performance.

  • Start with strong source content: The AI is finding the best moments inside your video, but it cannot create compelling moments that are not there. Content that is naturally energetic, specific, and quotable will produce stronger clips than content that is vague or monotone. Think about what moments in your content would make someone stop scrolling before you record.
  • Deliver clear value quickly: The clips that perform best on short-form platforms are usually the ones where the value or insight is apparent within the first few seconds. When you are recording, practice getting to the point faster — it will improve both your long-form content and the clips the AI extracts from it.
  • Focus on audience pain points: Content that directly addresses a problem your audience has tends to generate stronger clips than general commentary. Specific, actionable moments — "here is exactly why X happens and what to do about it" — are far more shareable than general observations.
  • Always review AI suggestions before publishing: The AI does excellent work at identifying candidates, but you know your audience better than any algorithm. Review each suggested clip, discard any that feel off, and lightly trim any where the start or end point could be sharper. Ten minutes of review before publishing is time well spent.
  • Use captions — and check their accuracy: Automatic captions are accurate most of the time, but accuracy drops with accents, technical terminology, or poor audio quality. Quickly scan the caption text before publishing to catch any significant errors that could undermine the clip's credibility.
  • Optimize your hooks: The first two to three seconds of a short clip determine whether someone keeps watching or scrolls past. If the AI-generated clip does not open on a strong moment, consider trimming it slightly so it starts with the most compelling part of the segment rather than the setup.
  • Publish consistently rather than in bursts: Spreading clips across the week produces better algorithmic results on most platforms than publishing multiple clips on a single day. Build a simple scheduling habit around your clip output to maintain consistent presence without overwhelming your audience.
  • Test different formats: If your platform of choice supports multiple short-form formats, test clips of different lengths. A fifteen-second clip and a sixty-second clip from the same moment will often perform differently — understanding which format your specific audience prefers helps you prioritize at export.
  • Use your clips to drive back to long-form: Always include a call to action in your clip description pointing to the full episode or video. The goal of short-form repurposing is not just to get views on the clips — it is to convert clip viewers into long-form subscribers. Make it easy for them to find the original content.
