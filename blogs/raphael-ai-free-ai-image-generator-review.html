Tips for Getting Better Results With Raphael AI

The difference between a mediocre AI-generated image and a genuinely useful one often comes down to how the prompt is written. Here are practical tips that actually improve output quality.

  • Be specific about your subject: "A red fox" gives you a fox. "A red fox sitting on a mossy log in a foggy autumn forest, looking directly at the camera, photorealistic" gives you something actually usable. The specificity is the signal the AI works from.
  • Describe the setting in detail: Where your subject exists matters as much as the subject itself. Background, environment, time of day, weather, and spatial context all shape the final image significantly.
  • Include lighting direction: "Warm afternoon sunlight," "soft overcast light," "dramatic side lighting," "golden hour glow" — lighting descriptions have an outsized effect on mood and visual quality in AI-generated images.
  • Specify the visual style explicitly: Even if you are also using the style selector, including style direction in the prompt reinforces the direction: "photorealistic," "watercolor illustration," "cinematic film still," "flat vector art," "oil painting style."
  • Name the mood you want: "Serene and peaceful," "high-energy and dynamic," "mysterious and moody," "warm and inviting" — emotional tone in the prompt influences the composition, color palette, and overall feel the AI reaches for.
  • Reference compositional framing: "Close-up portrait," "wide establishing shot," "aerial view," "eye-level perspective," "rule of thirds composition" — compositional language helps the AI understand how to frame the image, not just what to put in it.
  • Add color palette guidance: "Muted earth tones," "vibrant primary colors," "monochromatic blue palette," "warm oranges and ambers" — color direction makes the result more cohesive and on-brand for specific creative projects.
  • Use quality descriptors: "High detail," "sharp focus," "professional photography," "studio quality" — quality descriptors push the generation toward more polished outputs rather than rough or casual-feeling images.
  • Avoid negatives in your prompt: Describing what you do not want often produces unpredictable results. Instead of "no blurry backgrounds," try "sharp focus throughout." Describe what you do want rather than what to exclude.
  • Iterate with purpose: When the first result is not right, change one specific element of the prompt rather than rewriting everything from scratch. Targeted refinement teaches you faster what variables matter for a given type of image.
  • Save prompts that work: When you generate something genuinely good, write the prompt down. Modular prompts that you have proven work for a specific style or subject become a reusable creative asset over time.
  • Match the style to the platform: Think about where the image is going. A blog featured image needs different visual qualities than an Instagram carousel slide or a presentation background. Tailor your style selection and prompt to the platform's visual expectations.
