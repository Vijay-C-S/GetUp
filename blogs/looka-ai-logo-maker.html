How Good Are the Logos, Honestly?

Most Looka reviews either oversell the output or dismiss it without nuance. The truth is more interesting than either extreme. The quality is genuinely above average for automated design tools — but it has specific strengths and specific gaps that are worth understanding before you commit.

What the AI Gets Right

Typography is the strongest element. Font pairings in Looka's output are consistently sensible. The system understands which typefaces complement each other, how weight contrast between a primary font and a secondary one creates visual hierarchy, and how letter-spacing affects the perceived personality of a mark. I saw very few awkward type combinations across my tests — which is actually harder to achieve than it looks.

Color relationships are professional. The generated palettes avoid common amateur mistakes like clashing hues, colors that are too similar in value, or accent colors that overwhelm the primary mark. For digital use especially, the color choices tend to be practical and versatile.

Layout variety matches real-world use cases. Because Looka generates horizontal, stacked, icon-only, and text-only variations of each concept, you end up with a set of assets that actually work across different applications. A website header needs a different format than a social media avatar, and having both already generated saves significant time.

Where the Output Has Limits

The icon library is shared. Looka's icons are curated and varied, but they're available to every user on the platform. Two businesses in the same industry, using similar keywords, could theoretically end up with similar-looking icons. For most businesses this isn't a real problem — logo recognition depends heavily on color, typography, and context, not just the icon alone. But if your brand competes specifically on distinctiveness or operates in a visual category where being instantly differentiated matters, this is worth factoring in.

Complex or layered brand personalities are hard to express. If your brand needs to communicate multiple seemingly contradictory ideas — say, innovative yet traditional, or playful yet authoritative — the AI tends to pick the dominant signal and optimize for that. The nuance lives in your copy and your broader visual system, not in the mark itself. That's a legitimate constraint, not a bug.

Custom illustration is out of scope. If your brand identity depends on an original mascot, hand-lettered wordmark, or bespoke illustration, Looka isn't the tool for that job. No AI logo platform is, currently. You'd need a human illustrator. What Looka can do is get you 80% of the way there with a clean, professional mark that represents your brand until you're ready to invest in something fully custom.

A Useful Evaluation Framework

When reviewing logos from any generator — Looka included — it helps to have objective criteria rather than just going with what feels nice on first glance. Here's the framework I used across my testing sessions:

  • Legibility at small sizes: Does the logo read clearly at favicon dimensions and on a mobile app icon?
  • Balance: Does the icon and wordmark feel like one unified mark, or does one element compete with the other?
  • Background versatility: Does it work on both white and dark backgrounds without losing detail?
  • Monochrome test: Strip the color — does the logo still communicate and hold together?
  • Edit potential: How close is it to great with just a few targeted tweaks?

This prevents the common mistake of falling for a logo that only looks good at one size, on one background, in color. A logo that passes all five of those tests is a logo that will actually work in the real world.

Understanding Your File Format Options

Logo files are one of those things nobody thinks about until they suddenly and urgently need the right one. Looka covers the essential formats, and understanding which one to use where will save you frustration later.

  • PNG: The everyday digital format. Transparent backgrounds, consistent color rendering across screens, works reliably everywhere online. Not suitable for large-scale printing because it's a raster format — enlarge it beyond its native resolution and quality degrades.
  • SVG: A scalable vector format that stays perfectly sharp at any size. The standard format for modern websites and app interfaces. If your developer is building your site, this is usually what they'll want.
  • EPS: The vector format that professional printers use. Banners, signage, packaging, merchandise — any large-format or high-quality print application will require EPS. If you ever plan to print anything beyond a standard business card, you need this file.
  • PDF: Useful for sharing with clients or vendors who need a viewable format without specific design software, and for basic print layouts.

The practical implication: if your business will ever extend into physical print — even something as simple as a banner or a uniform — the premium package that includes vector files is the correct choice. The basic PNG-only package is a reasonable starting point for purely digital businesses, but most brands outgrow it quickly.

Seven Tips for Getting the Best Results from Looka

The platform rewards deliberate use. These adjustments made a consistent difference across my testing sessions:

  1. Be as specific as possible with your industry category. The more precise your category selection, the more relevant the AI's icon and color suggestions become. Vague categories produce average results; specific ones produce focused ones.
  2. Limit yourself to three or four style picks. More than five dilutes the AI's focus and produces a less coherent gallery. Pick your three strongest aesthetic preferences and commit to them.
  3. Search for icons laterally, not literally. Your most obvious keyword often produces the most generic icons. Try metaphors and related concepts — the second or third keyword you try frequently yields more distinctive results.
  4. Treat the first output as a draft, not a final product. The real work happens in the editor. Adjusting spacing, font weight, icon scale, and layout proportion after you've picked a concept produces significant improvements over the raw generated version.
  5. Run two or three separate sessions with different inputs. Fresh sessions generate completely different directions. Compare the strongest options across sessions before deciding, rather than optimizing within a single run.
  6. Test at favicon dimensions before purchasing. Shrink the preview or open it in a small browser tab. A logo that becomes unreadable at small sizes will fail in a lot of real applications — website browser tabs, app icons, social media thumbnails.
  7. Check the logo in black and white. Color is powerful, but a logo that only works in color is a fragile logo. If the mark loses its shape and readability without color, it needs more refinement before you commit to it.

Common Mistakes People Make on Looka (And How to Avoid Them)

Speed is one of Looka's strengths, but that same speed can lead to decisions you'll regret. These were the most consistent mistakes I observed across testing and feedback from other users:

  1. Falling for the first impressive result. The first gallery always contains a few options that look great on first glance. Save everything promising, walk away for an hour, and come back. Your choices will shift, and the logo you're most confident in after reflection is the one you'll still be happy with in six months.
  2. Choosing a trendy font over a timeless one. Trendy type ages quickly and conspicuously. Clean, well-spaced classics will look right longer. When in doubt, choose the font that would look appropriate two years from now.
  3. Using an icon with too much detail. Intricate icons look impressive at full size and become indistinct blobs at small sizes. Simpler marks with clear shapes scale better and read faster.
  4. Buying too quickly. The platform makes purchasing very easy, which can work against you. Spend at least 15 minutes editing before you commit. The edit pass almost always produces a noticeably better result than the raw generated version.
  5. Skipping the dark background test. Your logo will appear on dark backgrounds at some point — social media, presentations, digital ads. If it disappears or looks muddy on dark, you need to build a light version before you need it.
